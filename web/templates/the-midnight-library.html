Book Summaries / Fiction

The Midnight Library

A speculative story about regret and the lives we imagine.

By Matt Haig

Reading note · October 9, 2026

RegretPossibilityConnection

At a moment of profound despair, Nora Seed enters a library between life and death. Each book offers a different version of her life, shaped by a decision she might have made differently. The journeys challenge her belief that one missed opportunity explains everything.

The limits of an imagined perfect life

Nora explores possibilities involving careers, relationships, and ambitions. A life that looks impressive from outside can carry difficulties she never anticipated. Changing one decision does not remove uncertainty or make every other part of existence fall into place.

Pay attention to what is already here

The story moves from comparing hypothetical achievements toward noticing connection and possibility in an imperfect present. Its emotional argument is that regret can conceal the value of ordinary relationships and future choices.

A question to carry away

Are we comparing our real lives with complete alternatives, or only with their most attractive fragments? The novel turns that imbalance into a fantastical experiment.

Plot outcome · Ending spoilers

Nora ultimately chooses to return to her original life rather than remain in an alternative. She discovers reasons to continue and approaches her future with greater openness.
